Read the differences with care. GEM-15 is a digital surface model, so buildings and canopy are part of its height. Over a city block or a forest it measures the top of the surface rather than the ground, so a positive difference against a bare-earth source there is signal rather than error. The reverse happens too: a 30 m source smooths a hilltop down and a valley up, which is a resolution effect and not a disagreement about the model. The GEM-15 demo archive covers the Barcelona box only, and it quantises to whole metres below zoom 14, so compare numbers at zoom 14. The three sources also use different vertical datums, which puts a metre or two between them on identical ground.

Click anywhere on a map to read the elevation from all three sources. Press 3D, or right-drag, to tilt the three panes together and compare the surfaces themselves.
